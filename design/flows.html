Design pass 2 · Flows · revised 19 Oct 2026

Koumara flows.

Five flows that decide whether Koumara is worth switching to: setting it up, answering a VIP, fixing a message that landed in the wrong lane, reading newsletters like a magazine, and finding anything I've bought. Each step lists what I see, what Koumara does, and what changes for VoiceOver users.

Builds on the design brief. Each flow is drawn on iPhone first because it's the tightest screen, followed by the Mac version. iPad gets its own section near the end.

Decided
  • Gmail and iCloud to start. Other providers can come later.
  • Koumara replaces Apple Mail: full client, can be the default mail app.
  • VIP replies are always drafted and never sent automatically.
  • Apple's on-device model sorts everywhere. oMLX on my Mac writes drafts, long summaries and search embeddings.

Flow 2 · VIP reply

Every reply drafted, nothing sent without me

Every VIP message arrives with a full reply already drafted, so the fast path is two taps from the lock screen. If I don't get to it, Koumara nudges me with the draft attached. It never sends on its own. When my reply promises something, that promise becomes a task in Action.

Flow 4 · Newsletter reading

Browsing newsletters like a magazine rack

Reading is the lane I'll spend the most unhurried time in, so it shouldn't look like mail at all. Newsletters get a shelf organized by publication, a reader view that throws away the email layout, and a queue that moves me from one issue to the next without going back to a list. Unread issues don't pile up forever: anything I haven't opened in two weeks is quietly cleared to All Mail.

Edge states

When things aren't available

Local AI means the model isn't always there: an older device, a phone away from home, a battery saver. Koumara should degrade by doing less, never by doing something wrong. This is how each case looks.

What these flows decided

Rules I'll hold the build to

  1. Generated text is always introduced as generated."Draft:", "Gist", "Generated draft". In the UI and in what VoiceOver says.
  2. Koumara never sends on its own.Replies are drafted automatically and sent only by me, with a 10-second undo on every send.
  3. Every lane decision can explain itself."Why is this here?" names the stage and the evidence, every time.
  4. Corrections ask once, then stay out of the way.One sheet per sender, and every rule is listed with where it came from.
  5. Promises become tasks, when I say so.A sent reply that commits to a date offers to create the matching Action item.
  6. Newsletters are read, not processed.Grouped by publication, shown in a reader view with trackers blocked, and cleared automatically when they go stale.

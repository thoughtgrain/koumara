Koumara.

A mail app for Mac, iPad and iPhone that sorts my inbox into lanes I actually think in, keeps my VIPs answered within minutes, and does all of its thinking on my own hardware.

Koumara is the Greek name for the strawberry tree, Arbutus unedo, which shows up in the myths around Hermes, the messenger. The tree carries flowers, green fruit and ripe red fruit on the same branch at once. That's the whole visual idea: one inbox holding mail at different stages of ripeness.

The model

Five lanes instead of one inbox

Apple Mail gives me one pile and a search box. HEY gives me three opinionated piles. I want the HEY idea, with lanes that match the four jobs I listed, plus a gate that keeps strangers out until I've said yes. Every message lives in exactly one lane. Moving a message teaches Koumara a rule for that sender, so the sorting gets better from my corrections instead of from guesswork.

Visual direction

Apple Mail's manners, HEY's opinions

The app should feel like it shipped with the OS: system materials, SF Pro, native sidebars, standard gestures, keyboard shortcuts that behave the way Mac people expect. The personality comes from color and from structure, not from custom chrome.

From Apple Mail

  • Three-column split view on Mac and iPad, collapsing cleanly on iPhone.
  • Translucent sidebar and toolbars using system materials.
  • SF Pro, Dynamic Type and system spacing. No custom fonts in the app.
  • Swipe actions, context menus and menu-bar commands where people already look for them.

From HEY

  • Each lane gets its own color and its own layout, tuned for its job.
  • The Screener: strangers ask permission before they reach me.
  • Reading and Purchases feel like a magazine rack and a ledger, not a mail list.
  • Strong, plain-spoken labels instead of folder jargon.

Color comes from the tree

Lane colors run from leaf to bark. Each one is paired with a glyph, so the lane is still readable without color. Both themes keep lane text at 4.5:1 or better against its tint.

Why tiny models win on the edge

Gist · on device
  • Small models answer faster because nothing leaves the device.
  • Structured output beats free text for app features.
  • Keep prompts short; the context window is the real limit.

Most of the features people ask for are classification with good manners. You don't need a frontier model to tell a receipt from a newsletter, you need a fast one that never makes you wait.

How the sorting works

Cheap rules first, the model only where it earns its keep

Apple's on-device model (the Foundation Models framework) is small, fast and private, but it has a short context window and it only runs on Apple Intelligence hardware. So I don't want to throw every email at it. Headers already tell me most of what I need: a newsletter announces itself with List-Unsubscribe, a VIP is a sender match, a stranger is an unknown address. The model handles the ambiguous mail and the extraction work, and a bigger local model on my own machine handles the jobs that need more room.

Every message · instant

1. Header rules

VIP list, Screener, List-Unsubscribe, List-Id, Precedence: bulk, schema.org Order markup. Deterministic, free, explainable.

Ambiguous mail · on device

2. Apple model

Guided generation returns a typed result: lane, confidence, task and deadline, or order details. Runs on every device, nothing leaves it.

Heavy jobs · my Mac

3. oMLX

Long-thread summaries, VIP drafts in my voice, and embeddings for search, through oMLX's OpenAI-compatible endpoint on my Mac. Optional. iPhone and iPad use it when they can reach the Mac.

Whenever I move mail

4. My corrections

Moving a message asks once: "Always put mail from this sender here?" Yes becomes a stage-1 rule. The model gets used less over time.

The shape I'd ask the on-device model to fill in. Typed output means no parsing of free text, and a low confidence score sends the message to the lane I last used for that sender instead of guessing.

@Generable
struct Triage {
  @Guide(description: "vip, action, reading, purchase, or other")
  var lane: Lane
  @Guide(.range(0...1))
  var confidence: Double
  var task: Task?        // "Sign lease renewal", due 2026-10-19T17:00
  var order: OrderInfo?  // merchant, total, items, tracking, return-by
}

VIP replies

Every reply drafted, nothing sent without me

Decided: every VIP message gets a full reply drafted automatically the moment it lands, and Koumara never sends anything on its own. "Quick" comes from the draft being ready, not from skipping me. A wrong reply to the people I care most about costs more than a slow one.

To keep things fast without auto-sending, Koumara nudges me instead: if a VIP has been waiting longer than a window I set per person, I get a reminder with the draft attached. Drafts can be regenerated shorter, warmer or more formal, and every draft is labeled as generated until I edit or send it.

Accessibility

Commitments before any pixels ship

  • Lanes for VoiceOverEach lane is a rotor entry and a heading, so I can jump straight to Action or VIP without swiping through the list.
  • Never color aloneEvery lane color travels with a glyph and a text label. Chips say "Draft ready", not just a red dot.
  • Dynamic Type to AX5List rows reflow to stacked layouts at large sizes instead of truncating sender names.
  • AI is labeledSummaries, drafts and extracted tasks are marked as generated, and the original text is always one action away.
  • Nudges are calmA VIP nudge is announced once with who is waiting and for how long. There are no countdowns, because nothing is sent automatically.
  • Keyboard first on Mac1–5 switch lanes, R replies, E marks done, S snoozes. Full Keyboard Access reaches every control.
  • Motion respects settingsLane transitions cross-fade under Reduce Motion and drop translucency under Reduce Transparency.

Before I build

Decisions and open questions

  1. Accounts: Gmail and iCloudDecided. Gmail syncs through the Gmail API with Google sign-in, which gives labels and fast change history. iCloud Mail syncs over IMAP with an app-specific password, since Apple offers no sign-in for third-party mail apps. Others can come later.
  2. Replaces Apple MailDecided. Koumara is a full client: compose, drafts, signatures, attachments, search, and the option to become the default mail app on Mac, iPhone and iPad.
  3. Local AI: oMLX on my MacDecided. oMLX serves an OpenAI-compatible API on the Mac, so drafts, long summaries and search embeddings go there. Apple's on-device model covers sorting on every device and drafts when the Mac can't be reached.
  4. VIP replies are drafted, never sent automaticallyDecided. Auto-acknowledge is gone. Nudges replace it.
  5. Who counts as VIP?Still open. My default: a hand-picked list, with suggestions from who I reply to fastest.
  6. Device floorStill open. Apple's on-device model needs Apple Intelligence hardware and the OS 26 releases. Older devices would only get header rules.
  7. Reaching the Mac from iPhoneStill open. On home Wi-Fi it's direct. Away from home it needs a private tunnel such as Tailscale, or drafts fall back to on-device.

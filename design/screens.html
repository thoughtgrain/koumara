Koumara screens.

Detailed screens, starting where Koumara should feel least like email: newsletters assembled into a magazine. After that come the other lanes on iPhone in light and dark, the large-text layout, and the specs I'll build from.

Builds on the brief and the flows. All mail on this page is sample content.

Reading · The Edition

Newsletters, bound into a magazine

Twice a day (7 AM and 6 PM by default) Koumara gathers every unread newsletter into an Edition with a cover, a contents page and sections. Nothing is rewritten. Each article is the sender's own text, typeset in New York with a real reading measure, drop caps and pull quotes. The magazine is in the layout, not in made-up content.

The cover storyThe unread issue from the publication I open most, weighted toward longer reads. Ties go to the newest.
SectionsThe on-device model tags each issue with a topic (Technology, Design, Ideas, City). Sections only appear when they have two or more pieces.
In briefShort digests like City Bike Weekly don't get full pages. Each issue becomes one paragraph with a bold run-in head, the way magazines handle news briefs.
Color and imagesEach publication gets a tint sampled from its own header color, clamped to stay readable. Lead images load only for publications I've allowed. Otherwise the cover is typographic.

iPad: the spread

Landscape iPad is the most magazine-like screen I have, so it gets real spreads. The cover runs the lead story across most of the width with the contents down the right. Articles set in two columns, with the gist and source details in the outer margin like sidenotes.

Mac: a reading window

Reading opens in its own window. Contents sit on the left and the article gets one generous column. The gist and source details float in the right margin beside the paragraph they describe.

Lanes · iPhone

The other lanes, light and dark

Same structure everywhere: native large title, a one-line summary, then rows built for that lane's job. Each screen is shown in both appearances. Lane colors only appear in glyphs and chips, so the screens stay calm in dark mode.

Large text

VIP at accessibility size AX3

At the largest text sizes, rows stop trying to fit on one line. Avatars drop away, the name and time stack, the subject wraps instead of truncating, and previews hide. Two VIPs per screen is the honest result, and that's fine: the content is what matters at this size.

Layout rules

Spacing16 pt side margins on iPhone, 20 on iPad, system list insets on Mac. Rows use 10 pt vertical padding, growing with Dynamic Type.
Reading measureArticle text is capped at about 66 characters per line on every device. Wider screens get margins and sidenotes, not longer lines.
ChipsCapsule, 12 pt semibold, always glyph plus words. Never more than two chips on a row.
Hit targets44 × 44 pt minimum on touch, including chips that act as filters.
